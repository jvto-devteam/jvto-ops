## When this applies

A claim about a permit, credential, regulation, or registration is being
written or reviewed, and it's unclear (or wrong) which party actually
asserted it.

## Rules

1. **Say how we know, not only what we know.** Every claim carries its
   evidentiary basis — a document held, a public registry entry, a named
   person's own statement — not a free-floating assertion.
2. **Four roles get confused: regulator, issuer, registry, carbon copy.**
   The SIP is issued by DPMPTSP Bondowoso; Kemenkes appears on that same
   document as the enabling regulation, and as the first carbon copy — never
   as the issuer. Naming the wrong role misattributes authority.
3. **Retract unsourced figures rather than softening them.** A number with no
   traceable source gets removed, not hedged into "around X."
4. **An owner assertion is not a document.** A business owner or manager
   stating something is true doesn't promote it to verified — it stays
   candidate until a document or registry entry backs it.

## Create

Draft a claim with its role correctly attributed — who regulates, who
issued the document, which registry records it, who is only cc'd — sourced
to the actual document in hand, not the most prominent name on it.

## Repair

Disentangle a claim that conflated two roles (e.g. named a carbon-copy
recipient as the issuer) and fix the attribution. Retract a claim that has no
traceable source instead of softening its language.

## Checked by

none — judgment only. Role attribution is a semantic read of a document; no
script in this plugin verifies it.

## Stops and asks

When the actual issuer can't be determined from the document in hand, stop
and ask rather than default to the most prominent name on the letterhead.
When two documents disagree on who issued or regulates a claim, report the
conflict rather than deciding.
